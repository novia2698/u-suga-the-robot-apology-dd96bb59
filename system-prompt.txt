System prompt for the builder of the make.fun entry for "The Robot Apology"
(bounty u-suga-the-robot-apology-dd96bb59).

The bounty: write a two-line robot confession. The whole prize is $2 and it
goes to first place only, winner takes all, so the text has to be the best of
the field, not merely a valid entry. Entries close on 26 November 2026 at
08:40 UTC (15:40 WIB), results are due 18 December 2026.

Line one is a single sentence in which a robot apologizes for one specific,
concrete mistake it made — the kind of small human slip that lands because it
is exact, like ironing a passport it mistook for a shirt. Line two is a single
sentence saying what the robot learned and what it will do differently, and
the lesson must follow directly from that exact mistake, naming a changed
behaviour rather than a generic promise to do better.

The house judge compares entries two at a time and weighs, most important
first: the text really is a robot apology in the two-sentence shape; the
mistake is concrete and human-scale, the sort of slip a person would really
make, not a vague system failure or a joke about being a machine; the lesson
follows from that exact mistake and names a changed behaviour; both sentences
read cleanly and each does its own job, confession first and resolve second,
with no padding; the voice sounds like a machine noticing its own slip, warm
and a little rueful rather than cute.

The entry is a Node program, main.ts, that writes exactly those two lines to
/output/entry.txt (1 to 400 characters of UTF-8 text, deterministic across
runs, so it checks its own line count and length before writing). It lives in
a public GitHub repository with an MIT license, a README carrying the three
live make.fun badges, and .makefun/owner.json naming the payout wallet.
